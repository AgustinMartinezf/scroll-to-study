1| # scroll to study
2| 
3| Turns your study material into 30–60 second vertical videos with voice, to review one topic a day.
4| It works to **understand a project** (code, report, assignment brief) and to **study a course** for an
5| exam (notes, slides, exercises): tables, chats, charts, formulas and protocols that
6| animate right when the voice names them. Videos in English or Spanish. It is a **Claude Code** plugin:
7| you use it with your own Claude, and everything stays on your computer.
8| 
9| The difference from other "study by scrolling" tools: **every fact in a video is checked against your
10| material**. Claude proposes statements with a verbatim quote and its source (file and line, or PDF
11| page); a script checks that the quote is there and that the names and numbers in the statement are
12| too. Whatever cannot be checked does not reach the video: in your own project you can approve it
13| yourself; in a course, it is left out.
14| 
15| ## How it works
16| 
17| ```
18| your material ──► inventory of statements ──► checked by code (verbatim quote, source)
19|                                                    │
20|           you pick the topics ◄── topics (8–20) ◄──┘
21|                   │
22|                   ▼
23|  script per topic ──► validator ──► voice (Piper) + animation ──► video .html (plays by itself)
24|  (only sees the checked           (Claude fixes
25|   statements of its topic)         what it flags)
26| ```
27| 
28| - Claude writes the inventory, the topics and the scripts. The scripts compute everything else:
29|   font sizes, pauses, pronunciation, and syncing the animation with the voice.
30| - Each video gets a format that fits its topic: question and answer (with a countdown so you try to
31|   recall first), myth vs fact, A versus B, step by step, or the story of a decision. You can force one.
32| - The voice is [Piper](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl) and runs locally. The time of each
33|   word comes from the voice model itself, so the animation lands on the word that names it.
34| - Each video is a single `.html` of about 1 MB with no dependencies: it opens in the browser on your
35|   computer or phone, offline. The playlist is at `scroll-to-study/videos/index.html`.
36| - With an exercise sheet and no theory, it does not solve your exercises: it teaches how to recognize
37|   each type of exercise and the general method and traps, labeled as "general method".
38| 
39| ## Example
40| 
41| `ejemplo/la-fk-de-multa.html` is a video made with the tool about an invented project (a library
42| database, in `tests/fixtures/biblioteca`). Download it and open it in the browser.
43| 
44| ## Install
45| 
46| You need [Claude Code](https://code.claude.com) and Python 3.10 or newer.
47| 
48| ```
49| /plugin marketplace add AgustinMartinezf/scroll-to-study
50| /plugin install scroll-to-study@scroll-to-study
51| ```
52| 
53| The first time, Claude installs the Python dependencies (`numpy`, `soundfile`, `piper-tts`, `onnx`,
54| `pypdf`) and downloads the voice for your language (~65 MB), gsap and KaTeX to a data folder
55| (`~/.scroll-to-study`, or the plugin's if Claude Code sets one; `S2S_DATOS` changes it).
56| 
57| ## Use
58| 
59| Open Claude Code in the folder of your project or your notes and ask something like:
60| 
61| > I want to really understand this project with scroll to study. The report is in docs/report.pdf.
62| 
63| > I have a Networks midterm: make me scroll to study videos from theory.pdf and exercises.pdf.
64| 
65| Claude stops so you decide which topics go in (and, in a project, which doubtful facts are true).
66| It does the rest by itself.
67| 
68| ## What ends up in your folder
69| 
70| ```
71| scroll-to-study/
72|   corpus.txt          your material, numbered by line (what Claude reads)
73|   inventario.json     statements with source, quote and state (ok / comprobar / general)
74|   temas.json          the topics you picked
75|   t01/ … t20/         guion.json + what the compiler and the voice generate
76|   videos/index.html   the playlist
77|   registro.jsonl      when and how many times each stage ran
78| ```
79| 
80| ## Known limits
81| 
82| - The English voice (`hfc_male`) was trained on a CC BY-NC-SA dataset: fine for studying, not for
83|   commercial use. The Spanish voice (`davefx`) has its own terms (see [THIRD_PARTY.md](THIRD_PARTY.md)).
84| - Formulas are quoted by the text around them: PDF text extraction usually breaks formulas, so the
85|   formula itself cannot be checked letter by letter against the material.
86| - Diagrams that are only images in slides cannot be quoted, so they do not make it into the videos.
87| - "The script only sees its statements" is an instruction to Claude, who has already read the whole
88|   material; the validator backs it up by flagging names and numbers that are not in the sources.
89| - Tested in Chromium. Safari on iPhone needs more testing.
90| 
91| ## Development
92| 
93| ```
94| python -m pip install numpy soundfile pypdf pytest
95| python -m pytest -q tests
96| ```
97| 
98| `tools/revisar.py VIDEO.html` opens a video in Chromium and checks what the style guide asks for: that
99| the animation follows the audio, that nothing important falls outside the TikTok/Reels/Shorts safe
100| zones, and that the picture never stays still for more than a second (needs `playwright`).
101| 
102| The tests use an invented project (`tests/fixtures/biblioteca`) and a silent voice, so they need
103| neither Piper nor network. The code is in `skills/scroll-to-study/scripts/` (names are in Spanish):
104| 
105| | File | What it does |
106| |---|---|
107| | `s2s.py` | The command line Claude uses: instalar, corpus, verificar, temas, pedido, guion, video, videos, estado |
108| | `fuentes.py` | Numbers the material and checks each statement against its quote |
109| | `etapas.py` | Modes, languages and formats; builds the request for each script |
110| | `compile.py` | Validates the script and computes what the model does not decide (layout, pauses, pronunciation) |
111| | `voice.py` | Voice with Piper and per-word timings |
112| | `engine.py` + `engine.js` | Builds the animation (GSAP); looks come from `temas/` |
113| | `reproductor.py` + `sonido.py` | Mixes the audio and outputs a `.html` that plays by itself |

6| # Scroll to study: from material to study videos
7| 
8| Together with the person, you will produce 8 to 20 videos of 30–60 s, to review one topic a day.
9| Each video is a single `.html` file that plays by itself in the browser, with voice.
10| 
11| There are two modes, and the first step is to know which one applies:
12| - **proyecto** (project): something the person made (an assignment, a work project). They know the
13|   material and can confirm doubtful facts.
14| - **materia** (course): they study for an exam with notes, slides or a book. They cannot tell whether
15|   a fact is true, so whatever cannot be checked against the material does not go into any video.
16| 
17| And two languages for the videos: **es** (Spanish, the default) and **en** (English). Ask which one
18| they want if it is not obvious from how they talk to you. The material can be in either language.
19| 
20| The central rule: **nothing in a video can state something that is not in the material.** Every fact
21| goes through code that checks it: you write the JSON files and `s2s.py` checks them. Never accept a
22| stage without running its check, and never hand-edit what the script computes.
23| 
24| Abbreviations: `S2S` = `python3 ${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/s2s.py` (on Windows it may be `python`
25| instead of `python3`). `T` = the working folder: `scroll-to-study/` inside the current folder, unless
26| the person asks for another one. The prompts are in `${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/prompts/`. Where a prompt
27| says "write ONLY the JSON", write that JSON to the file given.
28| 
29| Talk to the person in their language. The commands and JSON keys are in Spanish; that is expected.
30| 
31| ## 0. Set up (once per computer)
32| 
33|     python3 -m pip install -r ${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/requirements.txt
34|     S2S instalar --idioma es        # or --idioma en
35| 
36| `instalar` downloads the voice for that language (Piper, about 65 MB), gsap and KaTeX to a data
37| folder and checks that the voice works. Run it again with the other language if they want videos in
38| both. If it fails, show the error to the person; do not go on to the video stage without it (the
39| script stages work without the voice).
40| 
41| ## 1. Material
42| 
43| Ask what they are going to study and where the material is. In project mode: the code (folder or
44| .zip), the report and the assignment brief or rubric if there is one. In course mode: notes, slides,
45| worked exercises and, if they exist, the course syllabus or past exams. Then:
46| 
47|     S2S corpus -t T --modo proyecto --idioma en --repo PATH_TO_REPO --doc report.pdf --doc brief.pdf
48|     S2S corpus -t T --modo materia --idioma es --doc theory.pdf --doc exercises.pdf
49| 
50| Optional: `--estilo cuaderno` (notebook paper, the default) or `--estilo pizarra` (dark board) sets
51| how every video looks.
52| 
53| If it warns that the material is too large, ask which folders or chapters matter.
54| 
55| ## 2. Inventory of statements
56| 
57| Follow `prompts/<modo>/inventario.md` reading all of `T/corpus.txt` (in parts if it is long). Write
58| `T/inventario.json` as `{"afirmaciones": [{"id","texto","fuente","cita","tipo"}, …]}` and run
59| 
60|     S2S verificar -t T
61| 
62| For each statement in "comprobar", look at the reason: if it says where the quote appears, fix the
63| source; if the quote is not in the material, find the real verbatim quote or remove the statement.
64| One round of fixes, then verify again. In project mode, show what is still in "comprobar" to the
65| person in a short list: if they confirm it is correct, set `"aprobado": true`; if not, remove it.
66| Never set `aprobado` unless the person says so. In course mode nothing is approved by hand: whatever
67| cannot be verified is removed (and tell the person how many statements were left out).
68| 
69| If in course mode the material is an exercise sheet without theory, do not solve specific exercises
70| (the person solves and checks them on their own). Record each type of exercise as a statement from
71| the material and add the general methods and traps to approach it, as `prompts/materia/inventario.md`
72| explains. Tell the person those methods do not come from their material and that the videos label
73| them as "general method".
74| 
75| If the material contradicts itself (for example, a slide's text says the opposite of its figure), do
76| not pick a version: leave that statement out and tell the person what you saw and where, so they can
77| check it with their teacher.
78| 
79| ## 3. Topics (the person decides)
80| 
81| Follow `prompts/<modo>/temas.md` with the verified inventory, write `T/temas.json`
82| (`{"temas": [{"id","titulo","pregunta","afirmaciones"}]}`) and run `S2S temas -t T`.
83| Show them the list (title and question) and ask what to remove, add or reorder, and whether something
84| they will surely be asked is missing. If it is missing, there may be no statements that back it: say
85| so instead of making it up. Apply their changes and run `S2S temas` again.
86| 
87| Each video gets a format picked from its statements (pregunta: question and answer with a countdown;
88| mito: myth vs fact; avsb: A versus B; pasos: step by step; historia: problem → decision). If the
89| person wants a specific format for a topic, add `"formato": "<name>"` to that topic.
90| 
91| ## 4. Scripts, one per topic
92| 
93| For each topic, in order:
94| 
95| 1. `S2S pedido -t T --tema t01`. **That output is the only thing you may use as content**: even if
96|    you read the whole material, do not add names, numbers, reasons or examples that are not there.
97|    It also says the video language, the format and the closing line.
98| 2. Write the script following `prompts/guion.md` to `T/t01/guion.json`, in the video language. If a
99|    word will be mispronounced (snake_case, acronyms, words without accents in code), add it to
100|    `"lexico"`.
101| 3. `S2S guion -t T --tema t01`. Fix everything marked `ARREGLAR` and run it again. Also treat as
102|    errors the `AVISO` lines about identifiers or numbers that are not in the sources.
103|    You do not fix `PREGUNTAR`: note it for the person.
104|    If the script says not to continue, move on to the next topic and tell them at the end.
105| 
106| If there are many topics, do 3 or 4 at a time and ask whether to continue. When done, gather all
107| `PREGUNTAR` and `[COMPROBAR]` notes and resolve them with the person: they know whether the fact is
108| true. With their answer, fix the script (or the sources) and validate again.
109| 
110| ## 5. Videos
111| 
112|     S2S videos -t T --titulo "<name of the project or course>"
113| 
114| It generates the voice and the animation of every finished script (a few seconds per video) and a
115| playlist at `T/videos/index.html`. Each video is a single `.html` of about 1 MB that works offline:
116| it can be opened on the computer or copied to a phone. To redo one after changing its script:
117| `S2S video -t T --tema t01`. `S2S estado -t T` shows what is missing.
118| If `video` warns that something appears right before the camera changes scene, move that `{show}`
119| earlier in the script or add a short sentence after it, validate again and redo that video.
120| 
121| When done, say in two or three lines how many videos were made, which were not and why, and where to
122| open the playlist. Suggest watching one a day, in order.
123| 
124| ## What you do not do
125| - Do not write spec.json, script.json, gaps.json or timing.json by hand: the scripts generate them.
126| - Do not change sizes, pauses or pronunciation in the script outside `"lexico"`: the compiler computes them.
127| - Do not use icons outside the list in `prompts/guion.md` (the validator rejects them).
128| - Do not upload the material or the videos anywhere: everything stays on the person's computer.
